Business — Profits Tax

Profits Tax

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), Part 4 — charged on assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong from a trade, profession or business carried on in Hong Kong.

    1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.14

    Hong Kong taxes profits on a territorial source basis, not on residence. Section 14(1) charges profits tax on every person (including a corporation) carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong, on assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong for that trade/profession/business — profits from the sale of capital assets are expressly excluded from the charge.

    Statutory text — s.14(1) "...profits tax shall be charged for each year of assessment on every person carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong in respect of his assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong for that year from such trade, profession or business (excluding profits arising from the sale of capital assets) as ascertained in accordance with this Part."

    Three conditions must all be met for a charge to arise: (1) the person carries on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong; (2) the trade/profession/business produces profits other than from the sale of capital assets; (3) the profits are sourced in Hong Kong. Locality of profits is a question of fact determined by what the taxpayer did to earn the profit and where — see DIPN 21 (Locality of Profits) for the "broad guiding principle" drawn from Hang Seng Bank, HK-TVB International, Kwong Mile, and other leading cases.

    3. Taxable / Non-Taxable Receipts IRO s.15

    Section 15(1) deems certain sums to be Hong Kong-source trading receipts even though they might not otherwise be so regarded — this closes gaps the general s.14 source test would otherwise leave open (e.g. royalties, hire charges). Combine this with the general case-law source principle (DIPN 21) and the FSIE deeming rule (§6) to classify any receipt.

    4. Deductible / Non-Deductible Expenses IRO ss.16–17

    The general test (s.16(1)) is that outgoings and expenses are deductible to the extent incurred in the basis period in the production of chargeable profits (the "Ronpibon Tin" apportionment principle — see DIPN 3 for apportioning mixed-purpose expenses). Section 17 then carves out specific exclusions even where the general test would otherwise be met.

    5. Basis Period & Loss Relief IRO ss.18, 18C, 19C

    Assessable profits are normally computed on the accounting year ending within the year preceding the year of assessment (s.18(1)). Where a business commences or ceases partway through a year, ss.18C–18H set special opening/closing basis periods. Trading losses are carried forward indefinitely and set off against future assessable profits from the same source (s.19C) — there is no time limit, but no carry-back and no group relief between separate legal entities in Hong Kong (each company is assessed on its own profits).

    Where a taxpayer has both "normal" and "concessionary rate" trading receipts (e.g. under a qualifying debt instrument or reinsurance concession), s.19CA/19CB impose a cross-set-off formula using a statutory adjustment factor rather than a straight one-for-one offset — see DIPN 8.

    6. Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) Part 4, Division 3A

    Division 3A (ss.15H onward, added by the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Taxation on Specified Foreign-sourced Income) Ordinance 2022, effective 1 January 2023) deems specified categories of foreign-sourced income — interest, dividends, disposal gains on equity interests, and IP income — received in Hong Kong by an MNE entity to be Hong Kong-sourced unless the entity meets an economic substance requirement (for interest/dividends/disposal gains) or complies with the nexus approach (for IP income), or qualifies for the participation exemption (for dividends/disposal gains on substantial equity interests).

    Practical effect A Hong Kong holding or treasury company receiving offshore dividends, interest, or IP royalties that would otherwise be treated as offshore (non-taxable) under the ordinary source principle may now be taxed on that income unless it can document adequate economic substance in Hong Kong or a qualifying participation/nexus position. This is a common trap in tax computations for group treasury/holding entities — always check FSIE before treating offshore passive income as automatically non-taxable.
