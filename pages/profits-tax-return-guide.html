Profits Tax Return Guide

Box-by-box walkthrough of BIR51 (current revision 4/2025) — bridging "what the law says" (the Profits Tax and Depreciation & Allowances pages) with "what you actually type into the form." Use the Box Finder to search by keyword instead of reading start to end.

Key structural point BIR51 does not contain a line-by-line add-back/deduction schedule as boxed items. Part 1 only captures the three final results — Assessable Profits, Adjusted Loss, Loss brought forward (boxes 1–3). The actual add-backs and deductions (depreciation, capital expenditure, non-deductible items, etc.) are worked out separately in a "tax computation with supporting schedules" that you attach as a Supporting Document — it is not printed on the form itself. That computation is exactly what the Computation Template on the Profits Tax page walks through.

1. Which Form Applies

FormWho files it
BIR51Corporations — the primary form for HK incorporated companies, and the focus of this guide.
BIR52Persons other than corporations — sole proprietorships, partnerships, limited partnership funds, bodies of persons. See §9 below for how it differs from BIR51.
BIR54Not a second "persons other than corporations" form — filed by a Hong Kong resident payer/agent to report a non-resident's HK-sourced profits under ss.20A/20B. See §10 below.

All three are issued annually and must be filed within the deadline stated on the Notice to File (IRC1952 for corporations, IRC1953 for others) — typically extended under the Block Extension Scheme where a tax representative is on record. Since 1 April 2023, all supplementary forms (S1–S22) must be filed electronically via www.ird.gov.hk/e_pfr.

3. Parts 2–3 — Tax Liability, Rate Election & Specified Matters

Part 2 computes Tax Payable/Repayable (IRD-computed, no entry box) and asks:

Box 4Chargeable at two-tiered rates? — the two-tiered rate election. If Yes, upload Form S1 (Part 9, box 42).

Part 3 is a run of Yes/No disclosure questions (boxes 5–15) covering non-resident IP payments (s.15(1)), fund/carried-interest deeming provisions, short/medium-term debt instruments, DTA relief claims, advance rulings, alternative bond schemes, regulatory capital securities, permanent-establishment status, and the s.51AAB e-filing mandate. None of these require a computed figure — they flag which supplementary forms and disclosures apply.

4. Parts 4–8 — Company Details & General Matters

Parts 4–6 are administrative: principal business activity + HSIC code (box 16), return-language preference, and an optional authorized representative section.

Part 7 (General Matters, boxes 17–36) is the section most likely to interact with your tax computation:

BoxWhat it asks
17–19Whether accounts are audited by law, by a HK practice unit, and the signing CPA's practising certificate number.
20Basis period From/To — feeds the basis-period rules (s.18/18C).
21–24Commencement/cessation of business, and what happened to the business/assets on cessation.

26–27Foreign-currency financial statements; private company status.

Part 8 (Transactions For/With Non-Residents, boxes 37–41) covers acting as an agent for a non-resident, IP value-creation contributions, and HK$ amounts for hire charges/professional fees paid to non-residents.

8. Parts 12–13 — Financial Data & Declaration

Part 12 (boxes 105–127) asks for headline financial-statement figures — gross income, turnover, inventories, gross profit/loss, dividend/interest income and expense, remuneration, commission, IP payments, management/consultancy fees, bad debts, net profit/loss per account, receivables/payables, issued share capital, and total assets. This is IRD cross-checking your return against the shape of your accounts, not a separate computation.

Part 13 (boxes 128–129) is the declaration — tick exactly one: box 128 for a Secretary/Manager/Director/Investment Manager/Provisional Liquidator/Liquidator declaring the return is complete and accurate, or box 129 for a Service Provider declaring it was prepared per the taxpayer's confirmed instructions (IR1476).

9. BIR52 — How It Differs from BIR51

Structurally parallel but shorter (129 boxes → ~111) and reorganized. The headline differences:

  • Part 1 extras (boxes 4–5): whether the proprietor/spouse/partner received emoluments or interest on capital from the business — such payments are non-deductible under s.17(2), and box 5 confirms the tax computation adjusts for it. The two-tiered rate election is box 6 here, not box 4.
  • Part 6 is entirely new: "Personal Particulars of Proprietor or Partners and Allocation of Profits/Loss and Claim for Deduction of Mandatory Contributions" (boxes 18–37) — per-partner HKID/BR number, profit/loss sharing ratio, a Personal Assessment election flag (the actual election is made on the individual's own BIR60, not here), allocated Assessable Profits/Adjusted Loss, and MPF contributions claimed. The allocation total must equal Part 1's box 1/2. See the Personal Assessment section.
  • Part 7 drops the corporation-only items (RBC regime, Companies Ordinance amalgamation, private-company/shareholder-change questions) and adds business-specific ones: cessation by death of the proprietor, and property purchase for building allowance purposes.
  • Part 9 lists only 10 supplementary forms (the corporation-only concessions S6–S14, S16–S18 don't apply).
  • Part 10 swaps BIR51's "interest to non-HK associated corporations" box for a "mandatory MPF contributions for proprietor/partners" deduction.
  • No separate depreciation-allowances Part — Financial Data shifts one part earlier, and the corporation-only "share-based payments"/"issued share capital" fields are dropped.
  • Declaration title varies by entity type: sole proprietorship → "person"; partnership → "precedent partner"; limited partnership fund → "general partner" / "investment manager"; body of persons → "principal officer"; no resident proprietor/partner → "agent"/"manager"; deceased estate → "executor."

10. BIR54 — What It Actually Is

Common misconception, corrected BIR54 is not a second "persons other than corporations" return. Its actual title is "Profits Tax Return — In Respect of Non-Resident Persons." It is filed under ss.20A/20B by the Hong Kong resident payer or agent, in the non-resident's own name, to report that non-resident's Hong Kong-sourced trade/business profits — most commonly royalty/IP payments, or income received via a Hong Kong agent. It's bilingual (Chinese/English) and far shorter than BIR51/52 (~22 boxes).

Key sections: a two-tiered rate election made on behalf of the non-resident (box 3, with the same connected-entity disclosure logic as BIR51/S1); an IP use/assignment block (boxes 11–17) determining whether the deemed-profit computation under s.21A(1)(a) applies 100% or 30% of the royalty receipt as assessable profit; a section on the agent's role in Hong Kong; and a reciprocal shipowner exemption block (boxes 18–20, s.23B(4A)) for non-resident shipowners.
