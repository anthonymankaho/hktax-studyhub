Business — Property Tax

Property Tax

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), Part 2 — charged on the owner of land or buildings in Hong Kong, on the net assessable value.

    1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.5

    Property tax is charged for each year of assessment on every person who owns land or buildings in Hong Kong, computed at the standard rate on the net assessable value — regardless of whether the owner carries on any trade or business. It is a tax on ownership + letting, not on trading profit as such.

    Statutory text — s.5(1) "Property tax shall...be charged for each year of assessment on every person being the owner of any land or buildings or land and buildings wherever situate in Hong Kong and shall be computed at the standard rate on the net assessable value of such land or buildings..."
    Current rate
    Standard rate applied to net assessable value15%

    Owner-occupied property generates no rental consideration and therefore has no assessable value — no property tax arises. Where the owner of the land is not the owner of the buildings on it, separate assessments are made for each (s.5(1)(b)).

    2. Net Assessable Value IRO s.5(1A), s.5B, s.7C

    Assessable value (s.5B(2)) is the consideration in money or money's worth payable for the right to use the land/buildings during the year — i.e. rent and other consideration receivable, not merely received. Net assessable value (s.5(1A)) then deducts:

    Assessable value (rent/consideration receivable for the year)X
    Less: irrecoverable consideration proved bad during the year (s.7C)(X)
    Less: rates paid by the owner, if the owner agreed to bear them(X)
    Less: statutory 20% allowance for repairs and outgoings (on the balance after rates)(X)
    Net assessable valueX

    The 20% repairs/outgoings allowance is flat and statutory — it applies regardless of the owner's actual repair costs, and cannot be varied by claiming higher actual expenditure. Consideration recovered in a later year, having previously been written off as irrecoverable under s.7C, is brought back into that later year's assessable value (s.7C(2)).

    Lease premiums paid up front are deemed under s.5B(3)/(4) to be payable in equal monthly instalments spread over the period of the right of use, capped at a maximum of 3 years — even if the actual lease term is longer. See the DIPN 4/DIPN 14 illustrations below.

    4. Corporate Interaction with Profits Tax IRO s.25, s.5(2)(a)

    For a corporation carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong, property tax and profits tax can otherwise double-tax the same rental income. Two relief mechanisms apply:

    • Set-off (s.25): where the rental profits form part of the corporation's trading profits (or the property is used to produce chargeable profits), profits tax payable is reduced by the property tax already paid on that property. If property tax paid exceeds the profits tax payable, the excess is refunded (s.25, proviso (b), via s.79).
    • Upfront exemption (s.5(2)(a)): instead of paying property tax and later claiming the set-off, a corporation entitled to the s.25 set-off may apply in writing to the Commissioner for the property to be exempted from property tax altogether. The corporation must notify the Commissioner within 30 days of any change in ownership/use affecting the exemption (s.5(2)(c)).
    Practical note Non-corporate owners (individuals, partnerships) do not get the s.25 set-off/exemption against profits tax in the same way — they may instead elect Personal Assessment (IRO s.41) to have property tax effectively absorbed into a single personal-assessment computation where advantageous. See the Salaries Tax page for Personal Assessment.
