Individual — Salaries Tax

Salaries Tax

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), Part 3 — charged on Hong Kong-source income from an office, employment of profit, or pension.

    1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.8

    Salaries tax is charged on income arising in or derived from Hong Kong from an office, employment of profit, or pension (s.8(1)) — again a territorial-source tax, not a residence-based one. Whether employment income is "Hong Kong employment" or "non-Hong Kong employment" is decided on the "totality of facts" test from Goepfert — the three key factors are: (1) where the employment contract was negotiated and is enforceable, (2) the residence of the employer, and (3) where the employee's remuneration is paid (see DIPN 10).

    The 60-day rule — s.8(1A)(b)/(1B) Income from services rendered wholly outside Hong Kong by a person not employed by the Government (and not ship/aircraft crew) is excluded from the charge — unless the person visited Hong Kong for more than 60 days in the basis period, in which case services rendered during those visits are brought back into charge.

    Directors' fees from a Hong Kong resident company are always Hong Kong-sourced regardless of where the director renders services. Income taxed in a DTA territory that gives a s.50 credit is excluded from the s.8(1A)(c) exemption route (must use the credit mechanism instead, not double relief).

    2. Rates & Standard Rate Cap IRO s.13, Schedule 2

    Progressive rates on net chargeable income (2025/26 and continuing into 2026/27 basis) — Schedule 2
    BandRate
    First HKD 50,0002%
    Next HKD 50,000 (50,001–100,000)6%
    Next HKD 50,000 (100,001–150,000)10%
    Next HKD 50,000 (150,001–200,000)14%
    Remainder (above 200,000)17%

    Under s.13, tax computed progressively on net chargeable income (i.e. after allowances) is capped at a standard-rate calculation applied to net total income (i.e. before personal allowances, but after concessionary deductions): 15% on the first HKD 5,000,000, 16% on the remainder. The taxpayer pays whichever of the two computations is lower — this matters most for higher earners with large personal allowances.

    One-off relief Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2026 (gazetted 22 May 2026): 100% reduction of salaries tax/personal assessment tax for YA 2025/26, capped at HKD 3,000. (Source: PwC, reviewed 22 Jul 2026 — verify against the gazetted ordinance.)

    4. Deductible / Non-Deductible Items IRO s.12

    The general test (s.12(1)(a)) is stricter than the profits tax test — expenses must be "wholly, exclusively and necessarily" incurred in producing the assessable income (not merely "to the extent"), and domestic/private expenses and capital expenditure are excluded outright. In practice very few ordinary employment expenses pass this test (see DIPN 9).

    6. Personal Assessment IRO s.41

    Personal Assessment is an election (not automatic) available to individuals (and, if both elect, jointly for married couples under s.41(1A)) that aggregates all sources of income — including property tax and profits tax from an unincorporated business — into one computation, deducting personal allowances and applying salaries-tax-style progressive rates. It is beneficial mainly where the taxpayer has property tax or unincorporated-business profits tax that would otherwise get no benefit from personal allowances, or has losses in one source that can offset income in another.

    Joint assessment (s.10(2), distinct from Personal Assessment) lets a married couple be taxed as one for salaries tax where beneficial — typically where one spouse has income below the value of their own allowances (DIPN 18).
