Question 1 — Mr Ivan Ho (15 marks)

Scenario15 marks

Ivan Ho is employed on an annual salary of $1,020,000 for the year of assessment 2026/27. During the year he incurred $60,000 on a job-related course, qualifying as a deductible self-education expense, and made the mandatory MPF contribution of $18,000. He is married; his wife has no income of her own.

Ivan also wholly owns a separate residential flat, which he lets out unconnected with his employment. Its net assessable value for the year (after rates and the 20% statutory allowance) is $200,000. He pays mortgage interest of $150,000 a year on the loan used to acquire that flat.

(a) Compute Ivan's salaries tax liability for the year, ignoring any election for personal assessment. (10 marks)

(b) Advise, with supporting calculations, whether Ivan should elect personal assessment for the year. (5 marks)

Question 2 — XYZ Trading Ltd (25 marks)

Scenario25 marks

XYZ Trading Ltd, a Hong Kong company with no connected entities, reports a net profit per accounts of $8,000,000 for the year ended 31 March 2027. The accounts include depreciation of $450,000, approved charitable donations of $200,000, and legal fees of $60,000 for acquiring a new five-year retail lease.

The company's plant and machinery pool (20% pool) had a written-down value brought forward of $600,000; during the year it bought qualifying plant for $800,000 and sold plant for $100,000. Separately, it incurred $1,500,000 of qualifying Type B R&D expenditure, already charged as an expense (and so already included) in the $8,000,000 net profit above. The company also has an unrelieved trading loss of $500,000 brought forward from an earlier year.

(a) Compute XYZ Trading Ltd's profits tax payable for the year. (20 marks)

(b) XYZ Trading Ltd is considering whether the $500,000 loss could instead be surrendered to a profitable fellow group company. Advise, with reasons. (5 marks)

Total for part (a): 20 marks. Stating the total enhanced R&D deduction ($4,500,000) instead of the additional amount still to be deducted ($3,000,000), and computing the annual allowance on the pool before deducting the initial allowance, are the two errors this question is designed to catch.

(b) (5 marks): Hong Kong has no group loss relief of any kind. Each company, including XYZ Trading Ltd and any fellow group company, is a separate person chargeable to profits tax in its own right (2 marks). The $500,000 loss cannot be surrendered to, or set off against, a fellow group company's profits, however profitable that company is or however closely the two are connected (2 marks). The loss simply remains within XYZ Trading Ltd itself, carried forward indefinitely with no time limit, to be set off only against XYZ's own future assessable profits from the same trade (1 mark).

Question 3 — Mr Anthony Cheung (15 marks)

Scenario15 marks

Anthony Cheung owns two properties in Hong Kong, both let in his own name during the year of assessment 2026/27.

The shop was let for the whole year at $45,000 a month. Anthony paid rates of $20,000 on it. The tenant fell into difficulty and three months' rent was proved irrecoverable during the year.

The flat was let for the whole year at $22,000 a month, and Anthony also received a premium of $540,000 on granting that five-year lease at the start of the year. The flat's tenant pays the rates direct to the Government.

(a) Compute Anthony's property tax liability for the year. (11 marks)

(b) Anthony is considering transferring both properties into a Hong Kong company he owns, which carries on a trading business here. Explain the property tax consequences. (4 marks)

Total for part (a): 11 marks. Each property is computed separately, with its own rates deduction and its own 20% allowance — aggregating the two into one computation loses marks. Spreading the premium over the full 60 months (giving $108,000) instead of applying the 36-month cap is the other error this question tests.

(b) (4 marks): A corporation carrying on a trade or business in Hong Kong may apply under s.5(2)(a) for exemption from property tax, on the basis that the rental income is instead brought into its profits tax computation (2 marks). If no such application is made, property tax is charged on the company in the ordinary way, but s.25 then sets that property tax off against the profits tax payable, so the same rent is not taxed twice either way (1 mark). Note the rate difference this creates: the rental profit would move from the flat 15% property tax charge to the corporate two-tier profits tax rates of 8.25%/16.5%, and the company could then deduct actual outgoings such as mortgage interest and repairs rather than being confined to the fixed 20% statutory allowance (1 mark).

Question 4 — Summit & Vale Partners (25 marks)

Scenario25 marks

Summit & Vale Partners is a Hong Kong partnership with two partners: Mr Vale, an individual, holding a 70% profit share, and Summit Holdings Ltd, a Hong Kong company, holding the remaining 30%.

The partnership's accounts for the year ended 31 March 2027 show a net profit of $6,400,000, after charging: a partner's salary of $900,000 paid to Mr Vale, a salary of $300,000 paid to Mr Vale's spouse (who works in the business), depreciation of $350,000, and approved charitable donations of $120,000.

The partnership's 30% plant and machinery pool had a written-down value brought forward of $500,000. During the year it bought qualifying plant for $900,000 and sold plant for $400,000.

(a) Compute the partnership's assessable profit for the year. (14 marks)

(b) Compute the profits tax payable by each partner, and in total. (8 marks)

(c) Mr Vale asks whether Summit Holdings Ltd could elect personal assessment on its share. Advise. (3 marks)

The $2,000,000 two-tier band is apportioned between the partners in the profit-sharing ratio, and each partner's slice is then taxed at their own rates — 7.5%/15% for the individual, 8.25%/16.5% for the corporate partner. Applying one flat set of rates to the whole $7,272,000, as if the partnership were a single taxpayer, is the error a mixed partnership is set to catch.

(c) (3 marks): No. Personal assessment is available to individuals only (1 mark). Summit Holdings Ltd is a company, so its share of the partnership's assessable profit simply stays in its own profits tax computation at the corporate rates, with no personal assessment route available to it (1 mark). Mr Vale, being an individual, could elect on his own share — but on these figures it would not help him: his share alone far exceeds the level at which the 17% marginal progressive rate overtakes the 15% standard rate, and there is no loss or interest here for the election to unlock (1 mark).

Question 5 — Mr Daniel Lau (15 marks)

Scenario15 marks

Daniel Lau is an individual resident in Hong Kong. In May 2026 he bought three adjacent mini-storage units in Kwun Tong for $3,200,000. He paid stamp duty and legal costs of $120,000 and spent $280,000 partitioning, repainting and installing access controls. He borrowed to fund the purchase and paid $180,000 interest during the holding period.

The units were never used by Daniel or let to tenants. He placed online advertisements for a resale before the renovation works were completed and sold all three units in February 2027 for $4,350,000, paying selling agency and legal costs of $90,000. Daniel says the gain is capital because he originally described the units as a "long-term store of value".

(a) Explain whether the gain is more likely to be taxable as profits from trade or non-taxable as capital. (10 marks)

(b) Assuming the gain is taxable as trading profits, compute Daniel's assessable profit and profits tax payable for YA 2026/27. (5 marks)

Show model answer

(a) (10 marks): The issue is whether Daniel was carrying on an adventure in the nature of trade, so that the profit arises from a trade, profession or business carried on in Hong Kong and is chargeable under s.14 (1 mark). Intention is relevant but is judged objectively from the facts, not merely from Daniel's label (1 mark). The subject matter points towards trade: three adjacent units are more consistent with a resale package than a personal long-term investment (1 mark). The short period of ownership, from May 2026 to February 2027, is a strong badge of trade (1 mark). The renovation and access-control works improved the units for onward sale rather than for own use or rental yield (1 mark). The early online advertisements, before completion of the works, show a pre-existing resale plan (1 mark). Borrowing and interest exposure support a need for quick realisation rather than passive holding (1 mark). There was no rental income or own use during the holding period, so there is little evidence of investment exploitation (1 mark). The buying, improving and selling activity was carried out in Hong Kong, so the source of any trading profit is Hong Kong (1 mark). On balance, the gain is more likely a taxable trading profit, and Daniel bears the practical burden of supporting any capital-gain claim if challenged (1 mark).

(b) Daniel Lau — Trading Profit and Profits Tax, YA 2026/27
Sale proceeds (1 mark)4,350,000
Less: purchase cost (1 mark)(3,200,000)
Less: stamp duty and legal costs (1 mark)(120,000)
Less: renovation and access controls(280,000)
Less: interest and selling costs(270,000)
Assessable profits (1 mark)480,000
Profits tax at the individual first-tier rate, $480,000 × 7.5% (1 mark)36,000

Total for part (b): 5 marks. This question is designed to catch the error of treating a self-serving "capital intention" statement as decisive, instead of weighing the badges of trade objectively.

Question 7 — Harbor Link Sourcing Ltd (15 marks)

Scenario15 marks

Harbor Link Sourcing Ltd is incorporated and managed in Hong Kong. During the year ended 31 March 2027 it earned net sourcing commission profit of $3,200,000 from overseas customers who bought electronic components from factories in Mainland China.

Harbor Link's Hong Kong staff found suppliers, negotiated prices and specifications, arranged purchase orders and dealt with quality complaints from its office in Hong Kong. Factory visits and shipment inspections in Mainland China were carried out by independent contractors engaged job by job. Customers signed their master supply agreements outside Hong Kong and paid Harbor Link into an overseas bank account.

(a) Advise whether Harbor Link's offshore claim is likely to succeed. (10 marks)

(b) If the whole profit is taxable in Hong Kong, compute the profits tax payable. (5 marks)

Show model answer

(a) (10 marks): The charge under s.14 is on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong from a trade, profession or business carried on here (1 mark). The company carries on business in Hong Kong: it is incorporated and managed here and its staff conduct the sourcing business from the Hong Kong office (1 mark). Source is decided by identifying the profit-producing operations and where those operations take place, not by asking where the customer is resident or where money is banked (1 mark). For a sourcing commission business, the relevant operations are finding suppliers, negotiating price and specifications, arranging purchase orders and handling performance problems (2 marks). Those core operations were performed by Harbor Link's own staff in Hong Kong (1 mark). The Mainland factory visits and inspections were ancillary execution work, and they were performed by independent contractors rather than by Harbor Link's own profit-producing team (1 mark). Signing master agreements outside Hong Kong is not decisive if the commercial work that earned the commission was done in Hong Kong (1 mark). The overseas bank account is administrative evidence, not a source rule (1 mark). Harbor Link bears the burden of proving the facts supporting an offshore claim under s.68(4); on these facts the claim is unlikely to succeed (1 mark).

(b) Harbor Link Sourcing Ltd — Profits Tax if the Offshore Claim Fails, YA 2026/27
Assessable profits3,200,000
First $2,000,000 at 8.25% (2 marks)165,000
Balance $1,200,000 at 16.5% (2 marks)198,000
Profits tax payable (1 mark)363,000

Total for part (b): 5 marks. This question is designed to catch the error of treating overseas customers, overseas signing or an overseas bank account as decisive, instead of applying the operations test to the profit-producing activities.

Question 8 — East Point Design Ltd (25 marks)

Scenario25 marks

East Point Design Ltd filed its profits tax return late after omitting a major service invoice. The Inland Revenue Department later issued an additional assessment on 15 August 2027, showing tax undercharged of $420,000, and requiring payment on 15 September 2027. East Point accepts that the invoice was taxable but says the omission was caused by a bookkeeper's spreadsheet error.

The directors are considering an objection because they also believe a separate deduction has been refused wrongly. They cannot pay the full assessment by the due date and expect cash will not be available until eight months after the due date.

(a) Explain the objection procedure and what East Point must do if it wants collection held over. (10 marks)

(b) Compute the maximum penalty under s.80 and the late-payment amount if the tax remains unpaid for eight months. (8 marks)

(c) Advise on the penalty exposure and the practical steps East Point should take now. (7 marks)

Show model answer

(a) (10 marks): A valid objection must be made in writing to the Commissioner within one month after the notice of assessment, stating precisely the grounds of objection (2 marks). Filing an objection does not by itself suspend collection: the tax remains payable unless the Commissioner orders holdover (2 marks). East Point should therefore request holdover at the same time, explaining the disputed deduction and providing supporting documents (2 marks). Holdover may be conditional, including requiring the taxpayer to purchase a tax reserve certificate or provide security (1 mark). If the objection is determined against East Point, the company may appeal to the Board of Review within one month after the determination (1 mark). The accepted omitted invoice should not be included in the objection grounds: East Point should separate the conceded undercharge from the genuinely disputed deduction (1 mark). Missing the payment date without holdover exposes the company to late-payment surcharges even while correspondence continues (1 mark).

(b) East Point Design Ltd — Penalty and Late-Payment Exposure
Tax undercharged / additional tax assessed420,000
Maximum s.80 penalty: $10,000 plus treble the tax undercharged (3 marks)1,270,000
First surcharge for late payment: 5% of $420,000 (1 mark)21,000
Further surcharge after six months: 10% of $441,000 (2 marks)44,100
Total surcharges after eight months65,100
Amount payable after eight months (2 marks)485,100

Total for part (b): 8 marks. The late-payment computation applies the second 10% surcharge to the tax plus the first surcharge, not to the original tax alone.

(c) (7 marks): The omission of taxable income creates penalty exposure even if the bookkeeper made the spreadsheet error: the company remains responsible for the correctness of its return (1 mark). A maximum s.80 penalty is not automatically imposed; IRD will consider culpability, cooperation, voluntary disclosure, previous compliance and whether prosecution or additional tax procedures are used (2 marks). East Point should make a full written disclosure of the omitted invoice, correct the computation, explain the control failure and provide evidence that the error was not deliberate (1 mark). It should pay the undisputed part as soon as possible, or at least seek instalment arrangements, because cash difficulty does not remove statutory surcharges (1 mark). It should support the disputed deduction with invoices, contracts, board approvals and accounting records, and keep the objection focused on that point (1 mark). Finally, it should improve controls over sales cut-off and return review before the next filing, since repeated errors aggravate penalties (1 mark).
