Business — Stamp Duty

Stamp Duty — Worked Illustrations

Every worked example backing the Stamp Duty page — drawn from IRD's SOIPNs and from Module 9: Principles of Taxation (4th ed.). This is the chapter needing the most correction of any topic: both source books were written before the 2023 AVD Scale 1 reform and/or the 2024 BSD/SSD rate cut, so most of their headline rates are now wrong — corrected figures are shown alongside the originals throughout.

A. From SOIPNs

Illustration — Provisional vs formal agreement timing SOIPN 1

Provisional agreement signed 1 March 2014, formal agreement signed 8 March 2014 (within 14 days) → only the formal agreement needs stamping, by 7 April 2014; the provisional agreement is duty-free. If the formal agreement instead follows more than 14 days later (e.g. 17 March), the provisional agreement itself becomes chargeable at full AVD by 31 March 2014, and the formal agreement is separately chargeable at a fixed $100 by 16 April 2014.

Illustration — AVD on a multi-asset instrument (residential + car parks) SOIPN 8, historical figures — pre-2023 flat-15% Scale 1

An HKPR buyer purchases a $13,000,000 residential property plus two car parking spaces worth $2,000,000 in one instrument. Scale 2 (3.75% at that value band) applies to the residential portion: $487,500. The car parks (non-residential, Scale 3) at the equivalent band: $150,000. Total AVD = $637,500. Historically (SOIPN 8, Nov 2018), a non-HKPR buyer paid a flat 15% Scale 1 on the residential portion instead: $1,950,000 + $150,000 = $2,100,000 — but Scale 1 is no longer flat; see illustration B1 below for how the same fact pattern is taxed today.

Illustration — Current graduated Scale 1 vs. Scale 2 Corrected from Cap. 117 First Schedule, post-2026 amendment

Same $13,000,000 residential property, non-HKPR (or corporate) buyer, no Scale 2 concession available: Scale 1 is graduated exactly like Scale 2 at this value band (3.75%), giving the same $487,500 — not a flat-15% $1,950,000. The two scales only diverge for a single residential property with consideration above $100,000,000: at $150,000,000, Scale 2 (HKPR sole property) still applies its 4.25% top rate = $6,375,000, while Scale 1 hits the new 6.5% flat band = $9,750,000 — a HKD 3.375 million difference driven entirely by the 2026 amendment's new top bands.

Illustration — SSD tiered by holding period SOIPN 5

Under the (now 0%-rated but still operative) post-27-Oct-2012 regime: a $3,000,000 residential property resold within 6 months would attract SSD at 20% ($600,000); resold within 6–12 months, 15% ($450,000); a $4,000,000 property resold within 12–36 months, 10% ($400,000). At the current 0% rate none of this is actually payable, but the tiering logic remains the statutory mechanism.

Illustration — BSD ignores which joint owner actually paid SOIPN 7

A Hong Kong permanent resident buys a $4,000,000 residential property jointly with an unrelated non-HKPR co-buyer. Because one joint owner is a non-HKPR and the two are not close relatives, BSD (at the applicable rate) applies to the full $4,000,000 value — it is not pro-rated to only the non-HKPR's share.

B. From Module 9: Principles of Taxation (4th ed.)

Adapted from a professional taxation qualification manual. Facts are paraphrased, not reproduced verbatim. This textbook predates the 2023 Scale 1 reform and the Feb 2024 BSD/SSD rate cut — every AVD/BSD/SSD figure below has been recomputed against current (2026) law, with the book's original figure shown for contrast.

Illustration B1 — AVD on residential + non-residential property in one purchase Module 9, Ch.7 §7.4

A Hong Kong company (never a HKPR) buys two properties on the same day: a commercial warehouse for $25,000,000, and a residential flat for $20,000,000 (consideration assumed to equal market value in both cases).

Original book figure used $3,000,000 (flat 15% Scale 1, the 2016–2023 rule); current law figure is $750,000 — a 75% reduction. The flat-15% Scale 1 was replaced by the same graduated table Scale 2 uses back in 2023; the 2026 amendment only adds new top bands above $100,000,000, irrelevant to a $20M deal.

Illustration B2 — Full AVD + SSD + BSD stack on a quick resale to a non-HKPR buyer Module 9, Ch.7 §7.6 — ss.24, 27

A residential flat is bought on 1 March 2018 and sold on 1 March 2019 (12 months + 1 day later) to a Mainland Chinese buyer who resides in HK ~183 days/year but holds no HK permanent ID — so he is not a HKPR for stamp duty purposes despite the residence. The stated price is $2,000,000 "subject to" an existing $5,000,000 mortgage, but market value at the (earlier, binding) contract date was $8,000,000. Because the transfer is at an undervalue and the debt is assumed, s.24 and s.27 deem the full $8,000,000 market value as the stampable amount for all three duties — that valuation mechanic is unaffected by any rate change.

Illustration B3 — Gift between close relatives: Scale 2 applies regardless of the recipient's residency Module 9, Ch.7 §7.6 — s.27, s.29AJ

A father gifts a residential flat (market value $10,000,000) to his son, a Canadian citizen with no HK permanent ID. Nil consideration is disregarded as a voluntary disposition — full market value is used. Because the transfer is between close relatives (parent–child), Scale 2 applies regardless of the son's residency status, and both BSD and SSD are exempt on the same close-relative basis.

AVD, Scale 2, "$6.72M–$20M" band, 3.75%$375,000
SSDExempt (close relative)
BSDExempt (close relative)

No correction needed — Scale 2 rates were not touched by the 2026 amendment, so the book's $375,000 figure is still correct today. Included deliberately as a contrast case: not every textbook figure in this chapter is wrong.

Illustration B4 — Hong Kong share transfer with failed group relief Module 9, Ch.7 §7.2 — s.45

A Hong Kong holding company transfers a 10% stake in an HKSE-listed (but overseas-incorporated) company to its 80%-owned subsidiary for $400,000, though market value is $500,000. Because the shares are HKSE-listed, they are Hong Kong stock for stamp duty purposes regardless of the issuer's place of incorporation. Group relief under s.45 fails — the parent only owns 80% of the subsidiary (below the 90% threshold), and an individual co-owner's personal stake cannot count toward that 90% test. The transfer at an undervalue is a voluntary disposition, so duty is charged on the higher $500,000 market value.

Stamp duty: $500,000 × 0.2%$1,000
Plus: fixed duty on the instrument of transfer$5

No correction needed — the 0.2% rate (0.1% buyer / 0.1% seller) matches current law exactly. Flag: elsewhere in the same textbook chapter, a rate of 0.26% is quoted — that was only correct for the temporary uplift period 1 Aug 2021–16 Nov 2023 and is now obsolete; 0.2% (as used in this illustration) is the figure to rely on.

Illustration B5 — Lease duty by term band (constructed — the textbook gives only the rate table, no worked figures) Module 9, Ch.7 §7.5, rate table only

A 5-year shop lease at $50,000/month rent, no premium. Term exceeds 3 years, so the 1% rate applies to average yearly rent.

Average yearly rent ($50,000 × 12)$600,000
Lease duty (1% of average yearly rent)$6,000

Rate bands (unchanged, current): 0.25% (≤1yr/undefined), 0.5% (1–3yr), 1% (>3yr) of average annual rent. If the lease is first executed as an "agreement for lease," that instrument bears this duty; a later formal lease on the same terms bears only a nominal $3 fixed duty.

Bottom line for this chapter Every AVD/BSD/SSD figure quoted in older study material — this textbook included — should be treated as showing the mechanic (which band, which test, which exemption) correctly, but the rate itself needs a fresh check against the Stamp Duty page's corrected tables before use in any real computation.
